# Memex

**Summary**: The Memex was a hypothetical personal knowledge machine proposed by Vannevar Bush in 1945 — a desk-sized device for storing and linking documents via associative trails, considered an intellectual predecessor to the LLM Wiki pattern and the hyperlink.

## The Concept

In his 1945 essay "As We May Think", Vannevar Bush described the Memex as a personal, curated knowledge store with **associative trails** between documents. The user could annotate documents and create named trails linking related material — trails that could be shared with others.

His vision was closer to the [[llm-wiki|LLM Wiki]] pattern than to what the web became:
- **Private** — personal, not public
- **Actively curated** — the connections were as valuable as the documents
- **Associative** — organized by conceptual links, not directory hierarchy

(source: What is LLM Wiki Pattern_ Persistent Knowledge with LLM Wikis.md)

## The Unsolved Problem

Bush described what the tool should do but couldn't solve who does the maintenance — who builds and keeps the associative trails current as new documents arrive. In 1945, there was no answer. The burden of curation made the vision impractical at scale.

The [[llm-wiki|LLM Wiki pattern]] answers this: the LLM handles all bookkeeping (cross-references, summarization, contradiction detection), leaving the human to supply sources and direction.

## Historical Significance

The Memex is considered a forerunner of:
- **Personal knowledge management** — tools like Roam Research, Obsidian, Notion
- **LLM Wiki** — AI-maintained knowledge bases with wikilinks

What changed from 1945 to 2026: LLMs can now be the tireless research assistant who does the filing.
